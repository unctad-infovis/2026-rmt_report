    For seaborne trade as a whole, UN Trade and Development (UNCTAD) forecasts flat volumes in 2026. Cargo is projected to travel farther on average. Ton-miles, which combine cargo weight and distance, are expected to rise 1.5%. For importers, longer voyages can mean later deliveries and higher costs.

    Importers can change suppliers as well as routes. As tariff conditions changed, buyers in the United States turned to suppliers in Viet Nam and elsewhere in Asia. The Viet Nam–US route accounted for 1.8% of global container trade in 2025, up from 0.5% in 2015.

    The China–US route, however, remained the world’s largest bilateral maritime container route despite a fall in Chinese shipments. Meanwhile, shipments from China grew on other routes, including those to India, Indonesia and Mexico.
